It balances. Two numbers in it are still wrong.

The balance check reads zero in all eight columns — not rounded to zero, genuinely zero, to 2.3 × 10−13. That is a real achievement and it is the hardest part of the build. It is also exactly why the two errors below survived: both of them feed the income statement and the cash flow statement with the same wrong figure, so the two sides move together and the check never complains.

A balanced model is not a correct model. That is the single most useful thing this rep can teach you.

Wrong figures the balance check cannot see

FINDING 1

Interest income is computed from a dead branch

Rows 248–250 of the Interest Income Schedule are hardcoded numbers, not formulas. They were a snapshot of the cash roll-forward at some earlier moment, and they have not moved since. The live cash roll-forward on the cash flow statement has moved. The two now disagree, and the gap widens every year.

FY2025 FY2026 FY2027 FY2028 FY2029 Cash flow stmt (r124) 137.269 161.178 188.710 215.077 252.857 Interest schedule (r250) 136.570 159.716 186.357 211.700 248.297 divergence 0.699 1.462 2.353 3.376 4.560 r176 "Cash Balance" =H250 ← the revolver tests the stale number too r253 "Interest Earned" = rate x AVERAGE(r249, r250)

Interest income therefore earns on a cash balance the model does not have. Because that wrong figure flows into net income and then into both retained earnings and the cash flow statement, the balance sheet still ties perfectly — the error is invisible to the only check you have.

Fix — point rows 248–250 back at the live statement: H248 =H122 · H249 =G250 · H250 =SUM(H248:H249), or delete the block and reference r122–124 directly.
FINDING 2

Depreciation is calibrated on opening PP&E and charged on closing

Your historical rate is computed correctly — E10 = −(E41/E11) divides depreciation by opening net book value, which is what note 4 of the report discloses. But the forecast applies that same rate to the closing balance: H210 = −H206*H211, where H211 is Ending PP&E.

FY2025 FY2026 FY2027 FY2028 FY2029 Depreciation charged 41.411 43.460 45.730 48.247 51.035 rate x OPENING PP&E 39.559 41.411 43.460 45.730 48.247 overstated by 1.852 2.049 2.270 2.517 2.788

Two consequences. Depreciation runs 1.9 to 2.8 too high every year, understating net income. And because closing PP&E is itself =SUM(opening, capex, depreciation), depreciation now depends on a figure that depends on depreciation — you have created a circular reference. Excel resolves it because iterative calculation is switched on in this workbook, so it never announced itself.

Fix — charge it on the opening balance: H210 = −H206*H208. The circularity disappears with it.
Dormant — wrong, but not firing yet

The revolver is broken and disconnected

FINDING 3

The repayment formula is wrong whenever the revolver has a balance

Your draw line is right. The repayment line is =MAX(MIN(0, cash−min), beginning), which returns the beginning balance whenever cash is above the minimum, and returns it again even while you are drawing. Then Ending =SUM(begin, draw, repay) adds it instead of subtracting.

begin cash min | yours d / r / end | correct d / r / end 0 120 60 | 0 / 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 / 0 same 0 40 60 | 20 / 0 / 20 | 20 / 0 / 20 same 20 120 60 | 0 / 20 / 40 | 0 / 20 / 0 wrong 20 40 60 | 20 / 20 / 60 | 20 / 0 / 40 wrong 50 200 60 | 0 / 50 / 100 | 0 / 50 / 0 wrong 30 55 60 | 5 / 30 / 65 | 5 / 0 / 35 wrong

Wrong in four of six cases — every case where the revolver carries a balance. It reads as correct today only because cash never falls below 60 in this forecast, so the revolver stays at zero and the formula is never exercised. Change one assumption and it produces nonsense.

Fix — cap the repayment and subtract it: Repay =MIN(begin, MAX(0, cash−min)) and Ending =begin + draw − repay.
FINDING 4

Even if it worked, the revolver is wired to nothing

The schedule computes a balance that no other statement reads.

Balance sheet r77 "Credit Revolver" H I J K L → all empty Cash flow financing r116-r119 LT debt · ST debt · equity · dividends no revolver line Revolver tests r176 = r250 the stale cash from finding 1

So a draw would never reach the balance sheet, never move cash, and would be tested against the wrong cash figure. Three separate breaks in one chain. There is also a third cash definition problem hiding here: the revolver must test cash before revolver activity, not final cash — otherwise, once you connect it, it will chase its own tail.

Fix — fill r77 from the schedule's ending balance, add a revolver row to financing, and feed the schedule cash before any revolver movement.

Credit where it is due

What this model gets right

This is a genuinely competent build, and several of the things below are exactly the items you have been missing for six reps running.

✓

It balances to 2.3 × 10⁻¹³ across all eight periods. No plugs, no forced numbers.

✓

You avoided the SG&A trap. SG&A grows at inflation off its own prior year, not as a percentage of revenue. That was the single most likely way to get this case wrong, and note 2 is exactly why. This is the best decision in the model.

✓

Working capital drivers are on the right bases. Receivables on revenue; inventory and payables on COGS; income taxes payable on the tax charge. Getting inventory and payables onto COGS rather than revenue is the detail most people miss.

✓

Income taxes payable is fully wired — a balance in current liabilities, sized in the working capital schedule, with only its change reaching the cash flow statement. Three reps ago this line did not exist.

✓

Historicals are hardcoded, forecasts are formulas, and the boundary is clean at FY2025. Interest expense keeps the reported figure in history and switches to the built-up figure in the forecast, which is the right call.

✓

Every historical ratio is derived, not typed. Growth, margin, tax rate, payable days and capex intensity are all live formulas off the statements — so the calibration updates if an input changes.

✓

Debt is modelled per tranche with separate rates, separate interest lines, and a total interest aggregation feeding one income statement row.

✓

Receivables days are modelled back toward the target in note 3 rather than held at the FY2024 actual. That is reading the case, not just the numbers.

✓

You picked up the commitment fee and the facility cap from note 5 and built rows for both.

These are defensible positions, not mistakes. But you should be able to say why — and for two of them I do not think you can.

The one analytical observation. Cash grows from 118.3 to 252.9 while the company pays 5.4% on term debt it is contractually amortising at only 20.0 a year. No sweep, no buyback, no step-up in dividends. That is a legitimate modelling choice, but it is a choice — and in a real review it is the first question you would be asked.

  2. Move depreciation onto opening PP&E. Net income rises, and a circular reference you did not intend disappears.
  3. Rebuild the revolver with the capped repayment, then connect it: balance sheet row 77, a financing line, and cash measured before revolver activity.
