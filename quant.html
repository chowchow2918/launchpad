Price & greeks

How to read the greeks

Delta — change in option value per $1 move in the underlying. Also a rough probability the option finishes in the money.

Gamma — how fast delta itself moves. High gamma means your hedge goes stale quickly.

Vega — value change per 1 percentage point of implied volatility.

Theta — value lost per calendar day, holding everything else fixed.

Rho — value change per 1 percentage point move in the risk-free rate.

Vanna — how delta shifts when volatility moves. Volga — how vega shifts when volatility moves; both matter when you are short volatility.

Efficient frontier

Optimal portfolios

What these two portfolios are

Minimum variance is the least volatile mix available, regardless of return. It is the leftmost point of the frontier.

Maximum Sharpe (the tangency portfolio) gives the most excess return per unit of risk. Combining it with cash traces the straight capital allocation line, which sits above the curve everywhere except where it touches.

Both are solved from your expected returns, volatilities and correlations — the answers are only as good as those three inputs, and expected returns are notoriously hard to estimate.

What this model assumes

Returns follow geometric Brownian motion: continuously compounded returns are normally distributed and independent from step to step. Volatility is constant.

Real markets are fatter-tailed than this, and crashes cluster rather than arriving independently, so treat the low percentiles as optimistic rather than conservative.

Because growth is multiplicative, the median ends up below the mean — a few very good paths drag the average up. The median is the more honest "typical" outcome.

Reading value-at-risk

VaR 95% is the loss that a bad period exceeds only 5% of the time. It says nothing about how bad things get beyond that point.

CVaR (expected shortfall) is the average loss given that you are in that worst 5% — the number that matters when the tail actually arrives.

Historical reads the cutoff straight off your data. Parametric assumes returns are normal. When the parametric figure is materially smaller, your data has fatter tails than a normal distribution, and the historical number is the one to trust.

What a move in yields does to the price

Duration, convexity and DV01

Modified duration is the percentage price fall for a 1 percentage point rise in yield — the first-order estimate of interest-rate risk.

Convexity is the curvature duration misses. Because it is positive for an ordinary bond, duration alone overstates losses when yields rise and understates gains when they fall.

DV01 is the dollar change in price for a 1 basis point move — the number a desk hedges against.
